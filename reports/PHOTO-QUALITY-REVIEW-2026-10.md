# Photo quality review, October 2026

The earlier media audit checked that each photo was real, correctly captioned and licensed. This second pass judged every one of the 330 photos on how it looks and whether it suits a US-first site for students: is the subject clear and well exposed, does a person cropped at the waist or a cluttered foreground dominate the frame, and is a better US example available?

## Glossary, gallery and atlas photos

- **People or poor composition replaced:** the seismometer installation (now a seismograph drum recording an earthquake), radiosondes on an office table (now a real Lake Charles sounding chart), a coal heap at an industrial site (now a coal seam in Ohio), a föhn photo with lens flare (now a chinook arch), and a dark K–Pg outcrop (now the marked boundary at Trinidad Lake, Colorado).
- **Atlas:**
  - Iowa: a featureless grey strip replaced by the Loess Hills.
  - Kentucky: power lines across the view replaced by Cumberland Falls.
  - Seven countries got landscape photos taken in the same country: Armenia (Lake Sevan), Belgium (Semois valley), Ecuador (Cotopaxi), Malta (Dingli Cliffs), Poland (Morskie Oko), Senegal (Lac Rose) and Ukraine (Carpathians).
- **Removed:** 13 photos that no longer appear anywhere, plus one unused museum photo.

## Kept on purpose

- Photos the user suggested earlier (Brodick sundial, Fulking spring line, Gleann nan Eun, Ko Kata Noi drawdown) stay even though some are small.
- No better in-country photo was found for Algeria, and the Cairngorms and Southsea case photos had no clearly better replacement.
